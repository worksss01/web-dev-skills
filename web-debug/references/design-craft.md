# Design craft across a page or flow

Use with [website design](website-design.md) for substantial creation/redesign. Reuse the chosen brand, stack, facts and scope while applying its modern-by-default contract. Classic/retro/legacy appearance requires the user's explicit style choice. Consult only relevant [reference mechanisms](design-patterns.md#beyond-the-opening) rather than loading a gallery. The goal is a resolved product-specific result; more effects, files or dependencies are not success criteria.

## Establish a sequence and relationships

Describe what the visitor should understand or do at the important moments of the page/flow. Choose a composition for each moment, with recurring visual rules and deliberate variation in scale, density, alignment or surface. Avoid choosing a generic section list first and filling it with unrelated copy. A strong opening needs a meaningful continuation, not the same layout repeated with different labels.

Convert a reference into transferable decisions: the dominant geometry, alignment anchors, text/media relationship, role of quiet space and supporting detail. Explain which relationship serves this task and what will change. Keep this in a short working note, not a new required schema. For example: a studio planner uses a quiet week grid for comparison, one accent for the selected day, overlapping events side by side, and a day timeline on narrow screens that retains the same selection and data.

Resolve typography and assets together. Choose the visual subject, crop, lighting and detail needed across the sequence; inspect the actual loaded font and language coverage. A hero asset should not be the only developed visual decision. Use a product-specific demonstration when appropriate rather than an unrelated animated object that could decorate any site. Do not invent product capabilities, metrics or proof to make that demonstration persuasive.

## Give operational information a purpose

State the decision supported by a dashboard, queue or comparison. Allocate space and emphasis accordingly. Define row anatomy, grouping, selection/detail context and action priority before decorating panels. A chart, badge or progress indication needs a meaningful relation to its data, units and state. Use consistent derived values for synthetic demonstrations; do not fill a metric panel with an unrelated sparkline. Preserve comparisons and important labels when density increases.

## Complete the interaction, not just its resting image

For relevant controls, inspect resting, hover/focus, pressed/dragging, selected and settled states. Coordinate the control and feedback: trigger/popup alignment, track/value relationship, progress/content change, and selection/detail context. Use native or established accessible behavior where appropriate; visual treatment can sit around it.

Apply the [selection surface contract](ui-design.md#selects-and-option-surfaces) as a completion requirement. A carefully styled closed select can still open an unrelated browser/OS picker; that fails the default modern-design requirement. Inspect the actual expanded surface and the task outcome after selection.

For meaningful motion, identify the trigger, the element retaining identity, the change in understanding/control and the settled result. Review intermediate, reverse and interrupted states. A menu opened and immediately closed should settle correctly; a slider should retain its value after release; a dialog should close and restore useful focus. Do not count an action as verified merely because a click command succeeded.

Keep content readable and tasks reachable throughout the flow. Avoid long empty scroll distances, fixed objects covering essential text, or content available only after animation. Maintain a complete reduced-motion/static route. CSS, SVG, canvas and WebGL are implementation choices, not quality levels; choose for the mechanism, browser targets and actual performance needs.

## Recompose narrow screens with the same meaning

Decide what stays primary, what becomes progressive detail and whether the representation changes. A week grid may become a day timeline, a vertical selector may become horizontal, or secondary row details may move into a panel. Preserve selected context, data meaning and access to the main task. When an overview becomes a detail view, keep a usable route to other dates or records; showing the current selection alone is insufficient. Do not leave a desktop sidebar occupying most of a narrow viewport, or hide controls visually while keeping them in the focus path.

Use real/representative content lengths early. Inspect the narrow active state as well as the empty or initial page. Controlled horizontal regions can be appropriate when their purpose and input access are clear; clipping the whole page or reducing text until everything fits is not an equivalent adaptation.

## Make critique actionable

Compare the rendered work with its intended relationships and relevant reference mechanisms. Identify the largest observed weakness: unclear focal idea, mismatched asset/type, repetitive sequence, weak task priority, incomplete state or narrow layout losing meaning. Correct that weakness and recapture affected views before adding decoration. Consider another composition when the first remains generic; do not merely recolor it or force arbitrary novelty.

Separate measured behavior, visual judgment and unverified source claims. Preserve useful mechanisms from a reference while correcting its observed limits; an attractive demo may still clip labels or omit keyboard behavior. Do not infer authorship from appearance, certify beauty from a score, or claim cross-model improvement from an author's walkthrough. Scale the work to the task and stop when its scoped criteria are met or a material limitation remains.
